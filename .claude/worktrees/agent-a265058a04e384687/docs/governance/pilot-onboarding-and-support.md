# Pilot onboarding, training and support materials

**Status: unapproved drafts. No pilot is authorised, no centre has been
onboarded, and no material here has been reviewed by anyone but its author.**

**Prepared by:** the repository maintainer, as preparatory material only.
**Last updated:** 16 August 2026.
**Review trigger:** whenever implemented behaviour, the role matrix or the public
information changes, and before any pilot is evaluated.

Read [the directory README](README.md) first.

## Why a deployed platform is not a ready one

A centre that receives working software and nothing else will invent its own
process, and the invented process is where the harm happens: a member of staff
who assumes someone is monitoring the queue, a coordinator who believes the
system decided a case was serious, a family told something before anyone checked
it.

Onboarding material is therefore not documentation for a product. It is the part
of the design that lives in people, and it fails in the same way software fails
when it claims more than it does.

## The rule every material follows

**It describes implemented behaviour, never intended behaviour.** The most
dangerous sentence in a training document is one that was true of a plan. Each
claim must be checkable against the product as deployed, and a material that
outruns the software is withdrawn rather than annotated.

**It states what Convive does not do, as prominently as what it does.** Convive
does not judge severity, does not decide what must happen, does not calculate a
protocol deadline, does not notify anybody outside the centre, and is not an
emergency channel. Every one of those is already stated in the product; the
training must not soften any of them.

**It never asks anyone to send report content through a support route.** A
support conversation is not a safeguarding channel, and a screenshot of a case
pasted into a support message is a data breach with a helpful intention.

## Materials required

### M-01 Centre onboarding checklist

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** The prerequisites a centre must satisfy before accounts
exist: who nominates the Direction role, which professionals are authorised, what
the centre has decided about its own protocol, and who at the centre owns the
relationship.

**Factual input:** account creation is administrator-driven with no
self-registration, activation uses a short-lived one-time link, and MFA is
mandatory for the Direction, Wellbeing and Protection, and Educational
Orientation roles. Those are recorded future-model decisions on #190, not
implemented behaviour, and any material must say which it is describing.

### M-02 Role-specific guidance

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** Short guidance for each audience, written to the approved
role matrix.

The three audiences need genuinely different things:

- **Reporters** need to know that no account is required, that anonymity is not
  absolute and why, that nothing they write reaches an emergency service, and
  how to get back to their own conversation with the secret they were shown once.
- **Professionals** need to know that seeing a case requires an explicit
  assignment, that a task template cites a source but decides nothing, and that
  a recorded task does not mean anyone was contacted.
- **Administrators** need to know that administering accounts grants no case
  content, that membership is not access, and what the audit trail records about
  their own actions.

### M-03 Operating modes

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** Material that distinguishes the three modes without
letting anyone confuse them: the public fictional demonstration, a fictional
pilot inside a real centre, and a real-data pilot.

**Why this is its own item:** the modes look identical from inside the interface.
The difference is entirely in whether what is typed is real, and that difference
is the whole safeguarding boundary.

### M-04 Support, incident and feedback routes

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** Three separate routes, and the rule that none of them
carries report content.

**Factual input:** the public contact addresses exist and forward to the
maintainer's mailbox. They are public correspondence routes and must never be
repurposed, which is stated in the vendor governance draft and repeated here
because a support document is exactly where that boundary erodes.

### M-05 Statement of limitations

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** An explicit, reviewed statement of what Convive does not
decide or replace, suitable for a centre to read before it commits.

**Available as input, not as approval:** the public information set already
states the boundary for the demonstration, and the product wording states it
inside the workspace. A centre-facing version says the same things with the
consequences spelled out for someone about to depend on it.

### M-06 Accessibility of the materials themselves

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** The accessibility standard the materials meet, and who
checks it.

A training pack that only works as a slide deck excludes the same people the
product works hard to include.

### M-07 Ownership, versioning and review

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** Who owns each material, how it is versioned, and the
rule that a change to implemented behaviour triggers a review of every material
that describes it.

**[DECISION REQUIRED]** Who provides support, during which hours, and what a
centre is told about out-of-hours. The honest answer today is that one person
maintains this project, and a material implying a staffed help desk would be the
same failure as a playbook implying someone watches the queue overnight.

## What stays true until these are answered

- No centre is onboarded and no pilot is authorised.
- No material is published or given to anybody outside the repository.
- No material describes behaviour the software does not have, and none softens a
  limitation the product already states.
- The public contact addresses stay public-correspondence routes.
